# The review gate: how to stop and hand over a decision

A review gate is a planned pause. Work stops, the owner looks, and nothing further is built until they answer. Every gate uses the same shape, so the owner always knows what is being asked.

## The shape (always in this order)

1. **What now exists.** One to three plain sentences. Name the files, pages or results.
2. **How to look at it.** The exact thing to open or do: a file name, a link, which page and what to click, what to try on a phone. Never "check it out" without saying how. It must be something the owner can do on the device they use: for someone who only uses a phone, a screenshot or a link that opens on the phone, never a command to type.
3. **What I checked, and what I did not.** Honestly. Name any check that failed, was skipped or could not be run. (See `references/honest-reporting.md`.)
4. **Decisions I need from you.** A short numbered list. For each: the question in plain words, **my recommendation** and the reason in one line, and what happens if they do not answer (only for decisions where waiting is harmless; for anything that sends, publishes, deletes, pays or changes real data, there is no default: it waits).
5. **Then wait.** End with what kind of reply moves things on ("Reply with your answers, or 'go' to accept my recommendations"), and stop.

Keep it short enough to read on a phone. If there is a lot to look at, say which one thing matters most.

## What counts as a yes

- A clear yes to **this** gate: "yes", "go", "looks good, carry on", or answers to the decisions asked.
- **Not** a yes: silence; a question back; "maybe"; a reply about something else; a yes given earlier to a different step.
- For an action that cannot be undone, the yes must be to that exact action with the target in front of the owner (see `references/before-outward-actions.md`).
- If the owner asks for changes that fit the story, make them (they are part of the same story), then show the gate again. Do not start the next story in between. If they ask for something beyond the story or the brief, offer a plan change, a new feature, or "later", and let them choose.

## Record it

Write the story's log entry after the owner answers, with their decisions in it (under "What changed" or "Open questions / follow-ups"), in their words where it matters. If a decision changes the plan, write the exact change under "Plan updates needed", make it once the owner says yes to it, and log a "Plan updated" entry.

## Example (invented project: a volunteer handbook)

> **What now exists:** a full outline of the handbook (`OUTLINE.md`): a welcome, the first-day checklist, three safety rules, and who to ask for what.
>
> **How to look at it:** open `OUTLINE.md`. It is one page; the safety rules are in part 3.
>
> **What I checked:** every part of the brief's success sentence has a place in the outline. I did not check the safety rules against the county's rules yet; that is Story 3.
>
> **Decisions I need from you:**
> 1. Should the safety rules come before the checklist? I recommend yes: people read the start most carefully. If you don't mind either way, I'll put them first.
> 2. Who is the weekend contact? I don't know this one, so it stays marked "to confirm" until you tell me.
>
> Reply with your answers, or "go" to accept my recommendation on 1. I'll wait.
