# Testing layers: documents, reports and processes

How to check work that is not software or analysis: a handbook, a report, a guide, a process, an automation. "Testing" here means reading against a checklist, checking facts at their source, and trying the thing for real. The short version lives in the project's rules file; this page explains each layer, gives an example, and says how much a project of each size needs.

## How much each size needs

| Layer | Lite | Standard | Full |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Checklist from the brief | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Facts and numbers | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Fresh-eyes read | one reader | ✓ | ✓, someone from the real audience |
| Review rounds | one round | ✓ | ✓, with named reviewers and dates |
| Try the process | when it is a process | ✓ | ✓ |
| Before handing over | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

## The layers

### Checklist from the brief
**What:** turn the brief's audience and success definition into a short checklist, and read every draft against it.
**Example (an invented volunteer handbook):** "A new volunteer knows where to go on day one; knows who to ask; can name the three safety rules."
**Break it:** read a draft that leaves out the safety rules; the checklist must catch it.

### Facts and numbers
**What:** every claim, figure, name, date, address and phone number checked against its source, with the source noted beside it while drafting.
**Rule:** never invent a fact to fill a gap; mark it "to confirm" instead.
**Example:** the weekend contact's number is checked against the current rota, not last year's.

### Fresh-eyes read
**What:** someone who was not involved reads it as the intended audience and says where they got lost, bored or confused.
**Tip:** ask them to do something with it ("tell me where you would go first"), not just to say whether they like it.

### Review rounds
**What:** each round has a named reviewer, a date, and a list of changes asked for; each change is made or declined in writing.
**Why:** it stops the same point being argued twice and shows who agreed to what.

### Try the process
**What:** for an automation or a procedure, run it once on harmless sample material and watch every step before using it for real. For an automation that sends, posts or deletes, the safety patterns apply in full (test copy first, try it once first, a fresh yes).
**Example:** walk through the first-day checklist with a volunteer on a quiet afternoon.

### Before handing over
**What:** read it once more from the top, check how it looks where it will really be seen (printed, on a phone, in the email), and write down what was and was not checked.
**Gotcha:** a document that looks right on a large screen can be unreadable on a phone or when printed in black and white; check where it will be read.
